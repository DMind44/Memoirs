The boat cut through the grey morning mist, its engine a low hum against the silence of the harbor. From the deck, Margaret could just make out the shape of the village — a handful of buildings clinging to the hillside like barnacles on an old hull.

It had been fifteen years since she'd last stood on this dock. The wood was newer now, the pilings straight, but the smell was the same: salt and diesel and something else, something she could never quite name but always remembered.

She made her way up the narrow lane toward the village center. The post office was still there, its red door bright against the white-washed walls. The bakery, too — though the sign had changed from "Peters" to "Costa." Some things remain, she thought, and some things become other things entirely.

The inn was her destination. The Lamplighter, it was called, and it stood at the top of the hill like a sentinel. Its windows glowed with firelight even in the middle of the day, and the smell of peat smoke reached her before she pushed open the door.

Inside, the common room was exactly as she remembered: low beamed ceiling, stone floor, a fire that seemed to burn eternally in the great hearth. The only change was the map on the wall — an old thing, its edges scorched, covered in markings she didn't recognize.

"That was my father's," said a voice from behind the bar. "He said it showed the way to places that don't exist anymore."
