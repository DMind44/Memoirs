Margaret spread the map across the desk, weighing its corners with whatever she could find — a magnifying glass, a coffee mug, a book of tide tables she hadn't opened in years. The paper was brittle, its surface a palimpsest of ink marks and penciled annotations in a hand she didn't recognize.

The coastline was unmistakable. She traced it with her finger: the same harbor, the same headland, the same cluster of rocks that the locals called the Three Sisters. But the interior was different — streams that no longer existed, paths that led to places she'd never heard of, and a series of numbered marks that seemed to correspond to nothing on any modern map.

She stood and walked to the window. The rain had started again, as it always did in this part of the world, streaking the glass and blurring the coastline into something almost abstract. The same coastline that stared back at her from the map, unchanged in its broad strokes, different in every detail.

The lantern on the windowsill caught her eye. It was old, brass, and it still worked — she'd lit it on her first night here, more out of curiosity than necessity. Its light had thrown strange shadows across the stone walls, shadows that seemed to move with a life of their own. She hadn't lit it since.
